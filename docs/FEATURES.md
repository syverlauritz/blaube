# Everything Blaube does

The full list, for the curious. The [README](../README.md) has the short, illustrated version.

## Sessions, blobs and play

- **What it's doing, at a glance.** While Claude thinks, its blob's thought bubble has a turning gear. While a tool runs, the bubble shows what kind: a magnifier for reading and searching, a pencil for editing, a flask for tests, a tick for a commit, arrows for a push or a pull, a parcel for installing, a bin for deleting, a folder, a globe for the web, a checklist for its to-do list, two little blobs for a helper, a plug for a connected app, and `>_` for any other command (`src/shared/actions.js`). When Claude keeps a to-do list, its blob wears it as a row of beads across its middle: filled in for the steps done, glowing for the one it's on. Each step ticked off gets a little hop ("2/5 ✓"), the last one a cheer ("plan done!") as it squeezes itself into a star for a moment, and hovering says where it's up to ("Plan: 2 of 5 · Writing tests"). A few moments get a little reaction: a hop and "Pushed!", a happy hop when tests pass, a wince when they fail, a nod for a commit, a sweat drop when it deletes something.
- **They talk.** A blob babbles what's in its speech bubble, in a cute made-up language through a talkbox, its mouth moving along: "okay!" with a 👍 when you send it a prompt (the 👍 pops up as the "-kay" lands; at most once a minute out loud, and not for prompts a loop or hook sends), a cheer when a turn you started is done ("Hurray!", "Ta-da!", "All done!", or "Yippee!" and "Woo-hoo!" after a long one), a line when it needs you (once per wait, never nagging), and its little exclamations ("hehe", "coffee?", "whoa!"). Nothing is said without a bubble saying it, so a reply in its talk box and the status bubbles stay quiet, and only one blob talks at a time. What you need to know always gets its bubble; the rest is rationed for the whole stage (a line of personality at most every three minutes, a word back when you play with one at most every half minute), so most antics say nothing. The speech bubbles are the size of the status bubbles, with the same pointed tail, in the fun font; everything else is Nunito. The babble is shaped like the words: one round syllable per syllable, with that syllable's vowel ("okay" is "oh-eh", "coffee" "ah-ee"), a "wah", "bl", "ba", hum or soft click from the consonant it starts with, stressed and final syllables longer, a question rising, an exclamation bright and bouncy. It's one soft synth note per line gliding between pitches through three resonant "mouth" filters (`src/renderer/voice.js`), all made on the spot, no sound files. Bigger blobs have lower, darker voices; helpers are higher. Tickle one (rub the cursor over it) and it giggles. Turn it off with **Sound** right in the right-click menu (a blob’s or Blaube’s), and set its **Volume** and **Wah at the start of each note** there too (or in Settings).
- **Choir.** **Sing together** (Blaube’s menu) and every blob sings a song together, in parts (`src/shared/choir.js`): one blob sings the tune, two add a bass ("bum, doo"), three an alto, four a tenor, five a high "oo" over it all, and more double the parts a hair apart, like a real choir. The biggest blobs take the bottom. They sway to the beat with their eyes shut and little notes float up. Songs (all public domain): Silent Night, Pachelbel's Canon, Ode to Joy, Twinkle Twinkle. Add your own in `src/shared/songs.js` (notes, beats, syllables and chords).
- **Real softbody physics.** Blaube is a ring of point masses held together by springs, gas pressure and shape matching. Every hop, bounce, squash and wobble comes out of the simulation. There are no canned animations. The defaults are tuned soft and light: 16 skin points, firmer edges, gentler pressure, no bounce, a steady grab.
- **Easy on the GPU.** Drawing is paced by motion, not by the screen's refresh: about 30 frames a second while breathing and working, up to 72 as things move faster (anything held, flung or flying, or your cursor moving near a blob, gets the full rate), and none while the screen is locked or the computer asleep. The physics keeps its own fixed steps and frames are drawn interpolated between them, so steady movement stays smooth at any frame rate (`paintNow` in `src/renderer/renderer.js`).
- **Toss it around.** Grab it anywhere: a damped spring pulls the spot you're holding toward the cursor, so the rest of the body dangles, stretches and swings. Fling it and it bounces off the screen edges, the menu bar, the macOS Dock (its real width, so it can sit beside or on top of it; on newer macOS that needs **Bump into the Dock’s sides…** in the menu, which asks for Accessibility so Blaube can read where the icons are), the Windows taskbar and its own little blobs.
- **One big blob per Claude Code session.** The first session takes over the big blob. Each new one splits off and grows to full size. A session's blob is named after its repository (the chat's name only where two sessions share one), and the mother is always the biggest blob, growing with her children. When a session ends, it hops over and merges into another one. The last one stays as Blaube.
- **One little blob per subagent.** When a session starts a subagent, a little blob splits off that session's big blob, named after the task it was given ("Ring Globe: Manta logo mode") and sized by the tokens it has used. Hover one for a single thought bubble: its task, its status, what it's doing and for how long ("Editing face.js · 1m 20s · 12k tokens"); hover a mother and she shows her latest thinking. When the subagent finishes, it hops back to its mother and merges in; one whose finish Blaube never heard about goes home once its own transcript says it's done. A helper that truly can't reach its mother (she's up on a window or a talk box too high to jump to, a wall's in the way, she's held out of reach) sighs, a heart breaks, and it rests a good while without trying, then goes to her happily once she comes to it; one that can get to her, however long it takes, never gives up. Subagents working in their own git worktree are recognised too.
  - **Working:** eyes squeezed shut in concentration, brows that now and then furrow or lift, and a thought bubble with a spinning gear while it thinks, or a `>_` while it runs a tool.
  - **Needs you:** worried face and a "?" bubble. It hops for attention until you look.
  - **Done:** happy face, bounces up and down with a "!" bubble until you look.
  - **You send it a message:** it nods and gives you a 👍.
  - Blobs only jump to get your attention. "Looking" means clicking the blob (to talk or to go to its terminal) or, on Windows, bringing the session's terminal window to the front. Each blob breathes at its own pace, gets sleepy (Z's) when nothing happens, and looks at your cursor when it isn't busy. The gaze is calm: looking around has a cooldown, it glides rather than darts, and a working blob stays focused. Moving the mouse never wakes a sleeping blob.
- **Which model, at a glance.** Its face says which model a session runs on: Opus has round dot eyes and soft oval blush, Sonnet tall eyes and blush lines, Haiku small eyes and freckles, Fable wide eyes and little hearts (hover for the exact model).
- **Hover a blob** to see its session's title, what it's doing right now ("Editing face.js"), and its git branch and model.
- **Size and strain.** A session grows as it uses tokens (its subagents' tokens count too), though a mother is never smaller than her children. As its context window fills, it puffs up and goes round and taut, a red flush spreads from its face, then it sweats, and near the limit it strains. A session working flat out (ten tools a minute or more) breaks a sweat too. `/compact` brings it back.
- **Effort.** Low effort zones out and drools while it works; high effort wears nerd glasses (a hard landing knocks them off, and it goes to fetch them, or drag them back yourself); max effort floats and meditates.
- **Looks.** Each session's blob wears its own little something: a curl, a bow, antennas, horns (one to five blunt, rounded triangles), cat ears, bunny ears or a sprout, randomly sized and placed, anchored steadily and swinging on a spring as it moves. About half of the blobs are plain, helpers too. Open sessions don't share a look.
- **Pets.** Every background task a session starts (a dev server, a watcher) becomes a little square pet that hops after its blob and shows a heart when it reaches it. Click a pet to see its command; drag it around. Pets play with each other, and wave goodbye (a hop and a heart) when their task ends.
- **Snack jar.** A little glass cookie jar on the floor shows your Claude usage limit: twenty cookies when your 5-hour session is fresh (or the week's, once that's over 75% used and closer to running out), one fewer for each 5% you use. The cookies are real little things inside: shake the jar, tip it or drop it and they tumble about, then settle into a new pile. Each time your usage goes up a cookie's worth, its lid pops and the top cookie of the pile arcs out into a nearby blob's mouth, nom, and the jar has one fewer; a bigger jump sends a few, one after another. Hover it for the numbers ("62% used · resets 20:20", the week, per model). Nearly empty, blobs nearby glance at it, worried; empty, they look hungry or nap until it resets and the cookies tumble back in. Drag it anywhere (a window top, a talk box); it stays there, per screen. Settings can hide it.
- **Arrivals.** A new session's blob only appears once Blaube knows its real state (transcript read, old background tasks checked against what's running), then pops out of the middle of the screen in its final colour, look and size. The others turn to look at the newcomer.
- **Feelings.** Thank a blob in a prompt (in the terminal or its talk box) and it blushes and squirms; tell it off and it looks sheepish; swear at it (anywhere in a message, English or Norwegian) and it's scared and sad: it flinches small, trembles, and says sorry in a small voice.
- **Don't shake too hard.** Hold a blob up and shake it: a few shakes make it dizzy; keep going and it swells up, cheeks full, holding it in, and turns green; keep going still and it heaves and barfs ("bleh!"), green splats on the floor that fade. A blob it lands on gets splats on it and pulls a face (squeezed eyes, zigzag mouth): "eww!". Then it slowly gets its colour back. (The hidden tuning panel can tone it down or turn it off.)
- **Play.** Rub the cursor back and forth over a blob or a pet to pet it (hearts!); a blob nearby may hop over wanting some too. Rest the cursor on top of a blob's head and it pushes up into it like a cat, eyes shut. Run the cursor round and round a blob and its eyes follow it round, until a few laps in it's seeing stars ("whoa…"). Rest it on a blob's face and it looks right at you; keep it up and it giggles and squirms. Fling a blob hard into another and the one that got hit sees stars; a gentle bump gets you a glare. Throw a helper at its mother and she catches it. Throw a blob at a calm one and it may brace for it, eyes wide, and catch it with a big squish, the two stuck together for a moment ("got you!"). Bowl one along the floor into two or more others and it knocks them silly and cheers ("strike!"). Helpers squish when a tool starts, rock while they think, glance at their family now and then, follow their mother if she's dragged far away, and celebrate with a heart before they merge back. Long turns get yawns, and a burst of hearts when they finish ("Done in 6m"), then a good long stretch, a yawn and a sway. A failed tool gets a wince; a compacted context gets a sigh of relief. Yawns are catching. Drop one onto another from high up and the one below gives like a trampoline and springs it back up ("boing!"). Drop a blob on another's head and the one below glares and shrugs it off (a helper on its mother's head gets to stay for a happy ride). Wiggle the cursor just above an idle blob and it wiggles back, then pounces; catch the cursor and a heart pops out. Carry one over and hold it gently against another for a moment and they hug: both go happy, the other leans in, hearts float up, and when you let go it gives a pleased little hop ("hehe"). Drag a blob into others and it shoves them along in one piece; one pinned against the edge squeezes out upwards. Ears and antennas twitch now and then and prick up when your cursor comes close. Blobs crouch before they jump and stretch out as they fly; fling one hard and it tumbles head over heels through the air, flopping back over if it lands on its side (and dizzy if it went round a lot), lean over toward a cursor that comes close, flinch when one whizzes past, and shift their weight, settle and look around when left alone. Press one and its skin dents in under the cursor; hold one still in the air and it wriggles to be let down; shake it and it gets dizzy, but swing it gently from side to side and it loves it ("wheee!"). They blink when they land, double-blink now and then, and go wide-eyed when startled. A helper resting against its mother sometimes nuzzles her. A failed tool or test lets some air out, and it sags for a moment. A hard landing sends a jiggle through any blob it's touching, and a blob running a tool tenses up in a little shiver now and then. At work its face follows what it's doing: reading, it scans along line after line; editing, it looks down and types in little bursts; on the web, it looks up and off to one side. When one tool runs a long while (a minute by default: a slow build, a long test run), it waits patiently, tapping a foot. Left alone long enough to doze off, blobs slump down soft and flat and breathe deeper, and wake with a big stretch when you come back. Asleep at night (10 pm to 7 am) they wear floppy nightcaps. Christmas week (December 20 to 26) they wear Santa hats all day, and in the first minutes of January 1 fireworks go off overhead, the blobs look up, and they all hop and cheer "Happy new year!". Fast asleep, a blob blows a little bubble at its mouth that swells and shrinks with each breath, and pops when it wakes with a start. Now and then it dreams in a little thought balloon of what it was last working on, and now and then it mumbles in its sleep, often about what it was last doing ("mm… fixed the login…"). A blob dozing off near another inches over to curl up beside it, and blobs resting together slowly fall into breathing in time. Late at night they get sleepier, dozing off sooner and yawning more. They remember how you've treated them for a few minutes: shake one and it's grumpy, leaning away and giving you a look when your cursor comes near, and now and then blowing you a raspberry ("pbbbt!", tongue out, buzzing all over); pet it and it's fond again, leaning in more and tagging along after your cursor for a bit. Every blob has its own character (some bouncier, more curious, fidgetier, blinkier or firmer than others), and they bob along as they talk or sing. Toss one high and it grins on the way up, goes wide-eyed "uh-oh" at the top, and screws its eyes shut to brace just before it lands. Come back to a blob after a while and it perks up to say hi; one that needs you makes big pleading eyes when your cursor is near. They're curious about windows, too: a window that appears gets a look (and a curious blob may hop over to see), and they watch a window you drag around. One that lands right at the edge of a window teeters: "whoa", a worried look down at the drop and a wobble back and forth over it, then a careful little hop back from the edge. One riding on a window you move wobbles like jelly on a plate as it starts and stops. Shake the window back and forth and it gets dizzy ("whoa…"). Sweep a window past fast and it blows the leaves along, and a blob by its leading edge leans out of the way. A session that's been waiting on you for a while (20 s by default) stops hopping on the spot and hops over toward your cursor instead. Two calm blobs sitting together now and then have a little chat, taking turns leaning in ("hm", "ooh", "haha"), and a mother leans in to catch a helper coming home. A bored blob now and then plays keepy-uppy with its task pet: the pet hops up onto its head and the blob bounces it up and catches it again and again, counting ("1!", "2!", "3!"), shuffling under it to catch it, until it misses ("aw") or sets a record. A bored blob now and then gives its task pet a playful shove; it tumbles off and scampers back, pleased. Task pets nap when their blob dozes, and once it's fast asleep one climbs up onto its head to nap there, hopping off when it wakes. Pick up a blob's task pet and it watches, worried, and hops up after it. Throw the pet and its blob chases after it; when it gets there the pet hops up onto its head ("got it!") and rides along as the blob brings it back to your cursor. Task pets feel for their blob: they rush over when it's seeing stars and bounce along when it's happy. A mother gulps a helper back in with a ripple and a little swell, and now and then a little "*burp*" after, with a blush (and maybe a giggle from a blob nearby). When a session finishes or starts needing you, the others look over, and calm ones nearby are pleased when one finishes (a long turn gets a little hop of applause). When the last busy session finishes and everything's done, they do a wave of hops along the row and the one at the end says "all done!". Two sessions that finish close together hop over to each other, jump, and bump in the air: "high five!". When a new session's blob lands, the nearest calm one hops over to say "hi!" and it says hi back with a bounce. When a session ends, the others turn to watch its blob head off, a little deflated, and the nearest one hops and says "bye!". Carry a blob around and the others watch it go; dangle it over another and the one below looks up, worried, and hunkers down. A blob flung past the others gets watched all the way, and one coming right at a blob makes it duck. While you type to a blob in its talk box it watches the box and bobs along, "mm-hm". Thank it in a prompt ("thanks!", "perfect", "nice work") and it blushes, squirms and sends up a couple of hearts ("aw, thanks!"); tell it off ("no, that's wrong") and it sags and looks sheepish ("sorry…"). A session that's still waiting on you after 3 minutes reminds you once, with what it's asking. Keep one waiting longer (8 minutes by default) and a little rain cloud gathers over its head and it slumps, glum, so the one you've kept waiting longest is easy to spot; point at it and the cloud puffs away. Hovering a waiting blob also says how long it's been waiting, and hovering a working one how long its turn has been going and what it's done so far ("So far: 3 files edited · 9 commands run · 1 failed", and what failed last; once it's done, "Did: …"), and a finished or idle one how long ago it finished ("Finished 3 min ago", "Idle for 2 h"); every one also tallies its day ("Today: 7 turns · 1 h 35 min of work"), and one that has changed files in its folder without committing them says so ("Not committed: 4 files (+120 −30)"). Scroll over a blob to squish it flat or pull it tall (keep going and it giggles). A working session that keeps hitting errors (4 failed tools or tests in two minutes by default) gets steamed up, little puffs rising off its head and a cross face, so you can spot the one going round in circles (and a calm blob nearby may hop over, lean in against it and cheer it on, "you got this!"); a green run or the turn ending calms it. When a session's context gets nearly full (85% by default) its blob tells you once, so you know to wrap up or compact. An idle blob that's awake while you're around sometimes amuses itself with three hops, each higher than the last (now and then the last one's a somersault, face and ears going all the way round, "ta-da!"); a calm one nearby may copy it hop for hop, a beat behind, its last one falling a little short ("copycat!"). With a few calm blobs about, one now and then leads a parade ("follow me!"): it hops off along the floor and the others fall in behind, hopping in step, single file, turning back at the edge of the screen. Two bored blobs side by side now and then play leapfrog ("leapfrog!"), taking turns hopping right over each other along the floor, the one underneath ducking down for it. A bored blob in a good mood now and then squeezes itself into a heart for a moment ("ta-da!"), balancing on its point, then lets go back into a blob. Now and then a bored blob puffs itself up like a balloon in a few gulps of air, each rounder and tauter than the last, until it floats up off the floor, cheeks full; then it lets go with a real zip about the screen, swerving and looping as the air rushes out ("pfffbt!"), and lands dizzy and giggling. A bored blob now and then does a roly-poly, rolling over along the floor with its face and ears going all the way round, and comes up grinning ("wheee!"; twice round and it's a bit dizzy). Two bored blobs side by side now and then stand back to back and stretch up as tall as they can ("who's taller?"); the taller one grins "me!", and now and then the shorter one cheats with a little hop ("ha!"). Two bored blobs now and then build a tower ("tower!"): one hops up onto the other's head and stands up tall there, wobbling to keep its balance ("ta-da!"), the one underneath bracing and grinning, until it hops down. With three bored blobs about, two now and then take the ends of a rope and swing it round while the one in the middle skips over it, counting ("1!", "2!", "3!"), until it's a beat late and trips ("oof!") or sets a record ("yay!"). Now and then bored blobs play hide and seek: one shuts its eyes and counts ("1…", "2…", "3…") while the others hop off to the far corners and crouch down low, peeking; then "ready or not!", and it hops about looking for them (sometimes the wrong way first) until each one it reaches jumps ("eep!") and is found. With three or more bored blobs about, one now and then shouts "dogpile!" and jumps on another, and the rest pile on after it, one on top of the next ("me too!"); the one at the bottom squishes down under them all, giggling, until the pile leans, slides and topples and everyone tumbles off laughing. Now and then two bored blobs play tag: one hops after the other, which hops away grinning, and a bump ("tag!") makes the other one it. A bored blob now and then blows a bubble and watches it float off; touch it with the cursor and it pops (and the blob giggles), or another blob may hop up and pop it first. Once in a long while one gets a fit of hiccups. Once in a while a calm blob sneaks up beside another and pops up tall, "boo!"; the other jumps, bristling all over like a startled cat, then glares (more pranks on Halloween, and the week before it a calm one now and then hops over to another for a "trick or treat!" (mostly a sweet gets tossed over and chewed, "yum!"; with "no sweets!" it's a "boo!"), another now and then pulls a sheet over itself and creeps up on a friend as a ghost, swaying, "boooo!", until the friend jumps ("eek!") and off comes the sheet ("it's me!"), and a little bat flits across now and then). They feel the time of day too: around noon one wonders about lunch (its tummy rumbles), mid-afternoon about coffee, on a Friday afternoon they hop about excited for the weekend, and first thing on a Monday one lets out a sigh. In autumn a few leaves drift down (cherry blossom petals in spring, snow in winter); they settle on the blobs' heads and on the ground, fall off when a blob moves, and a blob that's collected a few gives a little shake to get them off. A leaf landing on one sometimes makes it sneeze them all off ("achoo!"), and a sneeze at a blob beside it blows that one back a step ("bless you!", or "eww!"); snow makes it shiver. In snowy weather a bored blob now and then takes a run-up and slides along the icy floor ("wheee!"), leaning back and wobbling for balance until it glides to a stop (or braces at the edge of the screen, "whoa!"), and two bored blobs now and then have a snowball fight, taking turns to scoop and lob one at the other; a hit splats and knocks the skin in ("hey!"). Land in a pile of them and they fly up (a bored blob sometimes takes a big jump into one on purpose, "wheee"); slide through and they get pushed along. Their eyes follow one drifting down close by. Now and then a bored blob leans over and eats one lying beside it, chomp chomp ("nom"). Sweep the cursor through them and they scatter, and a task pet jumps to bat at one drifting down nearby. In summer a butterfly flutters by now and then and lands on a blob's head; the blob holds still, looking up at it, until it moves or your cursor comes close and the butterfly flies off (and a bored blob may hop up at one going past). They notice the computer, too: step away for a while (no keyboard or mouse anywhere, 5 minutes by default) and when you're back the sessions that finished or need you come over, and one says what changed while you were gone ("While you were away (12 min): api: done, web: needs you"); first thing in the morning it's a big stretch all round and a "Good morning!", with how yesterday went ("Yesterday: 12 turns · 2 h 30 min of work"). Typing in a terminal counts as being there, so they don't doze off while you work. When the screen wakes they stretch awake with it, plugging in the charger sends a fizzy tingle through them and a little hop, and on a low battery they go soft, saggy and tired. Every one of these is a force into the body, tunable in the **Life** sections of the tuning panel.
- **Windows are ledges.** Drop a blob (or throw it) so it comes down onto the top of another app's window and it lands softly and stays there; anything going up, or standing in front of a window, passes through. Move the window and whoever is on it rides along; flick it up and they fly off it; yank it away fast and they hang in the air for a surprised moment; close it and they fall. Helpers and pets jump up after their blob. Up there they mind the edge (a mother wanders off now and then), and a blob up on a ledge that wants your cursor hops down and comes over. Crowd too many blobs together and they grumble and spread out, one at a time: some hop up onto a window top (or a talk box) with room on it, and come down again when it gets crowded up there. Talk boxes are ledges too (the flat of the bubble's top): a blob can sit on one, its own included, and rides along when you drag the box by its header; close it and they fall. Turn the other windows' off with **Land on top of other windows** in Settings. On Windows a small PowerShell helper lists the visible windows; on macOS a JavaScript for Automation loop reads them through CoreGraphics, which needs no permission (`src/main/windows.js`).
- **Bubbles have weight.** Every bubble chases its spot above its blob on a spring and is leashed to it by a short tail (or, for a thought, two trailing puffs).
- **Click a session's blob** (or one of its little ones) to **talk to it**. A big, readable chat box opens with the conversation (what you typed in the terminal, what you sent from the blob, and Claude's replies, with a little formatting) and a field to type in. Your message reaches the session even if you're nowhere near its terminal: a background Stop hook waits for it and wakes Claude up (`src/main/inbox.js`). While it works, little dots and what it's doing right now ("Editing face.js") sit at the end of the log (after ten seconds, with how long it's been at it: "Editing face.js · 1m 20s", as the terminal counts), with a row under them for each helper it sent off: what it's on, and its time and tokens as the terminal counts them. The rows list the same helpers whose blobs are out. If Claude is busy, the message goes in at its next step ("at its next step", like typing into the terminal while it works; you can unsend it until then), or when the turn ends if it isn't using tools. Between messages the box shows what Claude did, a quiet line per step ("Read face.js", "Edited talk.js", "Ran npm test · 2 failing": a test run says how many failed or passed, from what jest, vitest, node --test, pytest, mocha, cargo, go, rspec or Playwright printed, and its blob says it too when tests fail; failures in red; tap one to see the change it made, or the command and what it printed, with a Copy for it; an image it looked at, such as a screenshot, shows right there), the last few of a long run with the rest a tap away. Under its name, its git status (branch, what's changed, what's not pushed yet), read locally and kept up to date while the box is open (tap it for the changed files; tap one for its diff, **Finder** (**Folder** on Windows) to see it there, **Edit** to open it in your editor, **Discard** (twice, to be sure) to discard your changes to it, as `git restore` would, kept in git's stash so **Put back** brings them back; a file in conflict has **Mine** and **Theirs**, one tap to keep that version and mark it settled, right even mid-rebase, where git's own ours and theirs are the other way round, and once none are left **Finish the merge** (or **Continue the rebase**) is first among its quick actions, git itself, right there, and **Call off the merge** (or rebase, two taps) takes it back to before it started; and its branches: tap one to switch to it once it's done (changes you haven't committed are set aside on the branch they were made on, and come back when you switch back to it), or **New branch…** to start one (its changes come along), changes you set aside yourself with `git stash` ("Set aside: try the tax idea · 2 days ago") with **Bring back**; and above them, for a repository on GitHub, **Open a PR on GitHub ↗** once its branch is pushed with no pull request open (GitHub's own page for it, no model call) or the branch on GitHub otherwise; and when some are already merged into main, "3 branches already merged into main" with **Delete them** (two taps; never the one it's on, one just made, or one with changes set aside); and its latest commits, which aren't pushed yet, and a tap on one for what it changed, file by file, as `git log` and `git show` would, and **Undo** on the newest when it's pushed nowhere, which takes it back with its changes kept, as `git reset --soft HEAD~1` would, and **Revert** (two taps) on the others, a new commit that undoes it as `git revert` would, history kept (a conflict leaves **Finish the revert** once it's settled); ## Hidden tuning panel

Press **⌘⌥⇧B** (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+B on Windows), or **Option/Alt + right-click** a big blob. Every parameter (colour, sizes, all the physics, dragging, hops, Dock collision, a physics debug overlay) has a live slider. **Copy config** puts the current values on the clipboard as JSON (the full config plus just what you changed), ready to paste back to Claude to bake in as the new defaults. **Paste config** applies a JSON blob. Settings are saved between launches.

**Show off** runs through the rarer bits of life in about a minute (a trick, a prank, a sneeze, hiccups, the charger tingle, a while-you-were-away note, a game of tag, a bubble, a butterfly, a trampoline bounce, a burp, a dream, a somersault, a tumble, leapfrog, a tower, a roly-poly, a heart, a balloon, skipping rope), so you don't have to wait hours to see them. **Sing** starts the choir on a song picked at random (**Stop singing** stops it), to hear the **Singing** section's knobs (loudness, attack, vibrato, softness, how much each singer is its own, the consonant ticks, the wah); a change is heard from the next song. Each section has its own **Reset**. The five **Life** sections (body, you, mood, together, world) hold everything that makes the blobs feel alive; every knob at 0 switches its behaviour off. If something feels off:

- **Too twitchy overall:** lower `idleFidget`, `leanToCursor` and `personality` first.
- **Hops feel slow to start:** lower `hopCrouch`. **Not springy enough:** raise `crouchDepth`.
- **Too stretchy in the air:** lower `flightStretch`.
- **Flinches when you just move the mouse normally:** raise `flinchSpeed`.
- **Dizzy too easily when you carry one around:** raise `shakeDizzy` (the number of quick direction changes it takes).
- **Comes over to you too soon (or not at all):** `seekAfter` (seconds of waiting) and `seekYou`.
- **Busy blobs look nervous:** lower `strain`.
- **Don't want them reacting to the computer:** `systemLife` to 0 (`welcomeBack` turns off just the "while you were away" note; `awayAfter` sets how long counts as away, `lowBattery` the level they tire at).

## No API tokens

Blaube never calls the Anthropic API. The physics runs locally, and session status comes from two local sources:

- [Claude Code hooks](https://docs.claude.com/en/docs/claude-code/hooks) that `curl` each event to a tiny server Blaube runs on `127.0.0.1:47821`. These give the richest status, including "needs you". If Blaube isn't running, the hooks just do nothing.
- `claude agents --json`, polled every few seconds, which lists the Claude Code sessions running on this machine as busy or idle. This works even before you connect the hooks.

Every Claude Code that Blaube starts (its own sessions, ones it opens in a terminal, resumed, forked or started on an issue, the usage check, sign-in, the `claude agents` poll) uses **your Claude login** (your subscription), never an API key: an `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` or `ANTHROPIC_AUTH_TOKEN` in the environment Blaube was started from is left out of theirs, so it can't quietly bill you by the token instead.

Only sessions running **on your computer** can show up. Chats in the Claude apps and cloud sessions (claude.ai/code, Projects threads) run on Anthropic's servers and can't reach Blaube.

### Checking the connection

```sh
grep -c blaube ~/.claude/settings.json          # hooks installed? (> 0)
curl http://127.0.0.1:47821/blaube/ping           # Blaube listening?
echo '{"session_id":"test","hook_event_name":"UserPromptSubmit","cwd":"/tmp/test"}' \
  | curl -s --data-binary @- http://127.0.0.1:47821/blaube/hook   # a "test" blob should split off
```

**Clicks on Windows (don't undo this).** The window's own mouse events can't be trusted on Windows: after a while it simply stops receiving them, even while it's set to catch clicks (seen in the logs many times). So:

- The window reports where grabbable things are (blobs, pets, dropped glasses) as boxes, a few times a second.
- The app polls the cursor 60 times a second and switches click-catching on over those boxes (re-applying it every half second), so clicks there never fall through to apps underneath.
- A small PowerShell helper watches the mouse buttons system-wide; the app turns presses that start over a blob into `pointer` events for the window, and sends the cursor position for dragging and for the eyes.
- Live reload (`npm start` while developing) always restarts the whole app; reloading just the window made clicks fall through.

On macOS and Linux the window's own mouse events are used. Problems that only show on a real desktop are logged to `blaube.log` in the app's data folder (clicks, the click-catching switch, going to a session's window).
